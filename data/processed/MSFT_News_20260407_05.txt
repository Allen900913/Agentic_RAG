Wedbush analyst Dan Ives expects Palantir (
NASDAQ:PLTR
), Oracle (
NYSE:ORCL
) and Microsoft (
NASDAQ:MSFT
) to lead the integration of artificial intelligence into defense operations.
Ives
described
defense AI as a long-term spending trend rather than a short-term pilot program. He said Palantir is likely the first mover in the space, while Oracle and Microsoft may capture infrastructure and enterprise layers.

Palantir's U.S. government revenue reached $570 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 66% from a year earlier. Oracle's cloud infrastructure revenue climbed 84% year over year to $4.9 billion. Microsoft's Azure business expanded 39% over the same period.
Ives framed the current geopolitical environment as driving a shift in military strategy that makes AI technology essential at the defense level. He said Palantir's Gotham platform is already embedded in intelligence workflows, giving the company an early position.
Oracle is building cloud infrastructure that supports the top five AI models, according to the note. Microsoft holds a roughly 27% stake in OpenAI and has about $250 billion in contracted Azure services.
The analyst suggested that defense AI spending could become a structural part of government budgets. He expects contracts to emerge as the fiscal 2026 budget process moves forward.
