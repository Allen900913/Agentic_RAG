[Title] For most of the last year, the bear case on

[Title] Apple (AAPL)

[NarrativeText] wrote itself.

[NarrativeText] Tariffs would gut margins. China was a lost cause. The AI story had no answer. iPhone upgrades had peaked.

[NarrativeText] Then last Wednesday happened.

[NarrativeText] Apple posted its best March quarter ever, with revenue of $111.2 billion (up 17%) and diluted EPS of $2.01 (up 22%).

[NarrativeText] Greater China, the segment everyone wrote off, grew 28%. Services hit another all-time high. The Board authorized another $100 billion buyback and raised the dividend 4%.

[NarrativeText] Search volume from financial pros spiked, with Apple drawing nearly 21,000 searches in the past month, per our TrackStar data, more than its next four hardware peers combined.

[NarrativeText] So is the run sustainable, or did Apple just front-load demand ahead of more tariff drama?

[Title] Apple’s Business

[NarrativeText] Apple needs no introduction, but the company’s transformation does. What was once a hardware story is now a hybrid: $80.2 billion in product sales last quarter alongside $31.0 billion in high-margin services.

[NarrativeText] The installed base of active devices hit another all-time high across every product category and every geography. Apple sells iPhones, Macs, iPads, wearables, and a growing suite of subscription services to roughly a billion paying users globally.

[NarrativeText] Apple segments its business into the following areas:

[Title] iPhone (51% of total revenues)

[ListItem] Flagship smartphone line, recently expanded with the iPhone 17 lineup and a new iPhone 17e

[Title] Services (28% of total revenues)

[ListItem] App Store, advertising, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+, and payment services

[Title] Mac (8% of total revenues)

[ListItem] Laptops and desktops, including the newly launched MacBook Neo

[Title] Wearables, Home & Accessories (7% of total revenues)

[ListItem] AirPods, Apple Watch, Vision Pro, and accessories

[Title] iPad (6% of total revenues)

[ListItem] Tablet lineup, recently refreshed with the M4-powered iPad Air

[NarrativeText] iPhone revenue jumped 22% to $57.0 billion, the standout of the quarter. Services climbed 16% to a record $31.0 billion, propelled by advertising, App Store, and cloud.

[NarrativeText] Greater China rebounded sharply, with renminbi strength adding fuel. Tim Cook called demand for the iPhone 17 lineup “extraordinary.”

[NarrativeText] On the strategic front, Apple is going all-in on AI infrastructure. R&D spending jumped 34% year-over-year to $11.4 billion last quarter, citing higher infrastructure costs.

[NarrativeText] Tariff overhang remains real but manageable. A February Supreme Court ruling struck down certain tariffs, and Apple is applying for refunds.

[NarrativeText] The company also flagged that semiconductor, NAND, and DRAM supply constraints could pressure costs into the next quarter.

[Title] Story Continues

[Title] Financials

[Title] Source: Stock Analysis

[NarrativeText] Apple’s trailing twelve-month revenue hit $451.4 billion, up 12.8% year-over-year, the strongest growth rate since FY 2021’s post-pandemic surge.

[NarrativeText] Gross margins continue to climb. TTM gross margin reached 47.9%, up from 46.2% in FY 2024 and 38.2% back in FY 2019, driven by the rising Services mix.

[NarrativeText] Operating margin now sits at 32.6%, and the company throws off $129.2 billion in free cash flow on a TTM basis.

[NarrativeText] That FCF easily covers the $7.7 billion in dividends and $37.0 billion in buybacks Apple executed in the first half of FY 2026.

[NarrativeText] The balance sheet is fortress-grade. Apple holds $146.6 billion in cash and marketable securities against $82.7 billion in term debt. Net cash position is positive and growing.

[Title] Valuation

[Title] Source: Seeking Alpha

[NarrativeText] Apple trades at a premium to nearly every peer on this list, with the exception of

[Title] Seagate Technology (STX)

[Text] .

[NarrativeText] On forward P/E, Apple sits at 33.5x versus

[Title] Sandisk (SNDK)

[Text] at 24.2x,

[Title] Western Digital (WDC)

[Title] at 29.7x, and

[Title] Dell Technologies (DELL)

[Title] at just 22.2x.

[NarrativeText] Price-to-cash-flow tells a similar story. Apple commands 30.7x, well above Dell’s 14.4x but cheaper than WDC and STX.

[NarrativeText] The market is paying up for Apple’s Services mix, capital returns, and brand moat. Whether that’s justified is the central question.

[Title] Growth

[Title] Source: Seeking Alpha

[NarrativeText] Here’s where the premium gets harder to defend.

[NarrativeText] Apple’s 12.8% YoY revenue growth trails SNDK’s 82.8% surge, WDC’s 32.0%, and STX’s 28.9%. Forward revenue growth of 9.6% is the weakest in the group except WDC.

[NarrativeText] Yet Apple’s three-year revenue CAGR of 5.4% beats WDC and SNDK, both of whom posted negative numbers over that stretch.

[NarrativeText] The hardware peers are riding an AI-driven storage upcycle. Apple is the steadier compounder.

[Title] Profitability

[Title] Source: Seeking Alpha

[NarrativeText] This is Apple’s home turf.

[NarrativeText] Apple’s 47.9% gross margin trails only SNDK’s 56.0%. EBITDA margin of 35.4% beats every peer except SNDK.

[NarrativeText] Levered FCF margin of 22.4% leads the group. Return on common equity of 141.5% dwarfs everyone except STX, which benefits from a tiny equity base.

[NarrativeText] Most striking: Apple generated $140.2 billion in cash from operations on a TTM basis. The next closest, Dell, did $11.2 billion.

[Title] Continued…

[Title] Our Opinion 7/10

[NarrativeText] Apple’s quarter was a genuine surprise to the upside, and the company’s cash generation remains in a league of its own.

[NarrativeText] The dividend hike and fresh $100 billion buyback give investors a tangible return floor.

[NarrativeText] But the stock isn’t cheap. At 33.5x forward earnings with sub-10% forward revenue growth, you’re paying a quality premium that leaves little room for an iPhone stumble or a tariff escalation.

[NarrativeText] For long-term holders, Apple stays a core position. For new money, we’d wait for a pullback before adding.
