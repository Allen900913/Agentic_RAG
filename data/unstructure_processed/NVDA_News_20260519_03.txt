[NarrativeText] Nvidia boss Jensen Huang expects China to eventually open its market to high-end US chips that can train and run artificial intelligence systems.

[NarrativeText] But he did not discuss sales of the powerful H200 model with top officials in Beijing, the businessman told Bloomberg Television in an interview broadcast Monday.

[NarrativeText] Huang travelled to the country last week with US President Donald Trump, who met Chinese President Xi Jinping.

[NarrativeText] The superpowers are in a fierce race for AI supremacy, and the H200 chip had until recently been barred from sale in China by Washington over national security concerns.

[NarrativeText] However, there is no sign that Chinese tech companies are buying them, as Beijing ramps up domestic chip development in a bid to challenge US dominance in the key sector.

[NarrativeText] "H200s are licensed to sell to China. But the Chinese government has to decide how much of their local market do they want to protect," Huang said.

[NarrativeText] "My sense is that over time the market will open," added Huang, CEO of Nvidia -- the world's most valuable company, due to huge demand for its AI hardware.

[NarrativeText] Trump said in December he had reached an agreement with Xi to ease the restrictions on H200 chips, a move some US lawmakers have warned could help the Chinese military.

[NarrativeText] Nvidia's most top-of-the-range offerings, the Blackwell and forthcoming Rubin series, remain banned for sale in China.

[NarrativeText] Xi told a delegation of US business executives on Thursday that China would "open wider" to the world.

[NarrativeText] "American companies will enjoy even brighter prospects in China," Xi said, Chinese state media reported.

[NarrativeText] Bloomberg Television asked Huang whether he spoke to Xi and Prime Minister Li Qiang about his chips.

[NarrativeText] "I didn't discuss directly with them about H200" although "President Trump had some conversations with the leaders", he replied.
